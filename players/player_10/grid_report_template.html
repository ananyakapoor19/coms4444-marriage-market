How to read the result

Positive rate is the fraction of Player 10 agents finishing with happiness above zero. Singles count as zero happiness. A bad marriage is a marriage with happiness at or below zero.

Each environment and seed receives equal weight. Happiness is also shown divided by its bonus, so larger bonuses do not overwhelm comparisons across environments.

The search is a coarse grid over the listed values. It identifies the best tested setting under this experiment; it does not prove a global optimum. Player 10’s source defaults were not changed.

Do the selected behaviors generalize?

The three highest-ranked global settings and each scenario’s training-selected winner were frozen before running seeds 9001–9005. The baseline replays the same fresh worlds.

EnvironmentPlayer codes × counts

g is the built-in greedy player; r is the built-in random player. Codes 1–10 refer to the corresponding group implementations. All Player 10 agents in a game use that game’s swept setting, including self-play.

  • Pairing: every setting replays identical engine seeds and seat orders within an environment/seed pair. Seats are shuffled deterministically to avoid always placing Player 10 first. Python and NumPy random streams are reset before each game.
  • Selection: settings rank by Player 10’s mean positive rate, with mean happiness divided by bonus breaking ties. Each scenario and seed is equally weighted; self-play’s larger number of Player 10 agents does not give it extra weight.
  • Coverage: @@GRID_ROWS@@ tuning rows and @@VALIDATION_ROWS@@ validation rows. Equivalent settings share one simulation, giving @@UNIQUE_GAMES@@ actual games. Validation includes the default, three globally selected settings, and the selected setting for each scenario.
  • Self-play interpretation: changing all Player 10 agents changes both our policy and the opponent population. These results assess homogeneous markets, not a single mutant policy against fixed-default Player 10 opponents.
  • Public information: singles-based decay uses the last singles count reported by choose_dates. Automatic repeat dates skip that method and can use a stale count.
  • Existing behavior: the collection phase still has its original inactive dated updates and Candidate-versus-ID membership check. This experiment preserves that behavior. Fresh-candidate selection still requests at least one unseen person when available, and known-positive candidates can fill the list first.
  • Generalization: only the mixed distribution and min(2, dimensions) monotonic traits were tested. Three tuning seeds and five validation seeds are a coarse screen; close differences need more independent seeds. The training-selected per-scenario winners are especially prone to overfitting.
